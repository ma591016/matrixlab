60-second lesson: What is a matrix?

A matrix organizes numbers into rows and columns. It can also act like a machine: give it a vector (an arrow), and multiplication tells you where that arrow goes. Some arrows keep their direction—those are eigenvectors.

60-second lesson: What is a quadratic form?

Think of x and y as two choices. A quadratic form assigns a height to each combination: q(x,y) = ax² + 2bxy + dy². The matrix controls the landscape. Level sets connect all choices with the same height.

60-second lesson: Why does profit become quadratic?

With linear inverse demand, selling more lowers price. Total revenue multiplies price by quantity, creating squared and cross-product terms. Subtract a cost that is linear in output (plus a fixed cost) and profit is a quadratic function whose curved part, −qᵀBq, is a quadratic form. Prefer other shapes? Click Build your own functions.

BRING THE THREE THEMES TOGETHER

One framework. Three perspectives.

01

Matrix fundamentals

You learn to manipulate a matrix and recover its special directions (eigenvectors), scaling factors (eigenvalues), inverse, and factorizations.

02

Quadratic geometry

The exact same A defines q(x) = xᵀAx. The eigenvectors set the principal axes, and eigenvalues control curvature and level-set shapes.

03

Business optimization

Two-product inverse demand and linear cost produce π(q) = (α − c)ᵀq − qᵀBq − F. The matrix B describes demand curvature, and capacity restrictions determine what output is feasible.
